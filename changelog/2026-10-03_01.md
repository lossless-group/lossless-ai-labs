## Why Care?

Most people deep in agentic coding are building alone. Each has a silo:
their own `CLAUDE.md` habits, their own way of handing context to an agent,
their own skills and slash commands, their own idea of when a plan is
worth writing down. Those silos grow up without seeing each other, so the
same problems get solved twice, differently, and nobody finds out which
way was better.

Our studies so far have looked outward in one direction. We pin a handful
(sometimes tens) of open-source repos that answer one question, and we
read them before deciding something. That works for "how do vector
databases rank recall?" It doesn't work for "a friend built their whole
agentic workflow differently from us. What should each of us steal?"

It also answers a question we keep getting. Some friends see The Lossless
Group as further down the rabbit hole and ask what they should take from
our setup. "Read our skills repo" isn't a good answer. A good answer is
grounded in *their* code and is short enough that they'll act on it.

## What's New?

- **A new kind of study: collaborations.** `studies/collaborations/` holds
  one folder per collaborator. Their codebase is mounted read-only inside
  it as a submodule, and our notes sit beside it.
- **An exploration that sets out the method:**
  `Explore-Collaborator-Codebases-for-Insights-Comparative-Highlights`, in
  `studies/context-v/explorations/`. It covers how this differs from a
  regular study, nine things to compare, what the output looks like, and
  the ground rules.
- **The first case is mounted.** A friend's app, which they shared with us
  for review, moved from the ai-labs root into its collaboration folder.

## How it Works

### Two directions, one pass

Each comparison produces a single **comparative highlights** note with
both directions in it:

| Section | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Convergences | Ideas both silos reached independently. The most trustworthy patterns |
| What we could take from them | Concrete, each with a path in their repo and where it lands in ours |
| What they could take from us | Tied to something we saw in their repo, ranked by payoff for *their* setup |
| Deliberate differences | Choices that are right for each context. Named so nobody "fixes" them |

The "what they could take from us" half stays to a handful of items. If
we hand someone twenty conventions, they'll adopt none.

### What gets compared

Agent setup (instruction files, commands, skills, hooks), context
management, packaged know-how, planning to shipping, verification, git
and history, architecture and stack, harness and model choices, and the
craft of the collaborator's own domain. That last one is often where we
learn the most, because it's furthest from our own work.

### Ground rules

- **Read-only.** We never commit, push, or branch inside their repo.
- **Ideas, not code.** We borrow patterns. If a snippet is worth copying,
  we ask.
- **Translate, don't transplant.** Our conventions grew up in a
  27-project pseudomonorepo. A single-app repo needs the smallest version
  of each.
- **"Ahead" is a single axis at best.** Being further into agent tooling
  doesn't make us better at their product or their users. We expect to
  learn at least as much as we teach.

## Under the Hood

We nearly made this a promoted study repo (`lossless-group/study-<slug>`
with nested submodules), like every other study in ai-labs. Two things
argued against it. A collaborator's repo is often private, so a study
repo nesting it couldn't be cloned in full by most readers. And there's
only ever one codebase per collaborator, so no nesting is needed. So
`collaborations/` is a plain folder in ai-labs:

```
studies/collaborations/<name>/
├── README.md            # what this is, ground rules, link to the method
├── <name>-codebase/     # their repo, an ai-labs submodule (read-only)
└── *.md                 # our notes and comparative highlights
```

The first codebase had been mounted at the ai-labs root earlier the same
day. We moved it with `git mv`, which carries the working copy and
rewrites `.gitmodules` in one step. We ran the tree's three relocation
checks first anyway: no unpushed branches, no local-only branches, and no
gitignored secrets.

We also don't name collaborators in changelog entries. These entries roll
up into public splash pages and get indexed. A friend who shared their
code for review shouldn't turn up in search results because of our ship
notes.

## What's Next?

- A first real reading pass on the mounted codebase, producing the first
  comparative highlights note.
- Open questions in the exploration: how to share the "what they could
  take from us" half (a note, a call, or a PR to their repo if they ask),
  and whether to invite collaborators to run the same pass on our silo.
