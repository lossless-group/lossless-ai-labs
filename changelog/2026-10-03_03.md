## Why Care?

If you build an AI product with coding agents, you're running **two
harnesses**, whether you've named them or not:

- a **developer-agents harness**: everything that helps coding agents build
  the software (instruction files, specs, changelogs, handoffs, CI)
- an **in-product-agents harness**: everything that makes the product's own
  agents produce good output (runtime prompts, output contracts, evaluation)

We only saw this clearly by comparing ourselves with someone else. A friend
building a spoken-assessment app with coding agents let us read their repo,
and we ran our [[2026-10-03_02|three-step relay]] on it. The first version
of the comparison looked like rough parity. It wasn't. We were pairing
their product machinery against our development machinery.

Split by layer, the picture flipped into something much more useful:

|  | Developer-agents harness | In-product-agents harness |
|---|---|---|
| **The Collaborator** | Thin | **Deep** |
| **Lossless** | **Deep** | Moderate |

Each side built depth where its pain was. They pushed hard on whether the
product's output is any good. We pushed hard on building software with
agents across dozens of repos. So the lessons run across the diagonal:
they can adopt our developer-harness habits cheaply, and their in-product
harness shows us how to take memo generation to the next level.

## What's New?

- **The comparison page:** `/studies/collaborations/silo-to-silo-01/` on
  the AI Labs splash. It opens with the 2×2 layer map, then harness maps,
  workflow loops and the four comparisons (shared, similar, different,
  missing) for each layer, then recommendations for both sides. The
  collaborator is anonymized throughout.
- **The study notes behind it:** an evidence inventory of their repo, with
  every artifact tagged by layer, and a two-way comparison with a ledger,
  eight diagrams, insights and ranked recommendations.
- **A sharper method.** The collaboration exploration and all three plans
  now separate the two harnesses from the start, and forbid pairing across
  them.

## How it Works

### What they can take from us (small, developer layer)

Their specs are real (signed off before building, updated after shipping)
and their commit messages read like a lab notebook. What's missing is
structure and discoverability. So the top recommendations are small: a
short root `CLAUDE.md`, a curated permissions policy instead of 200-odd
click-approvals, one CI job, and a handoff at the end of every session.
None needs our pseudomonorepo machinery.

### What we can take from them (bigger, in-product layer)

Their exam pipeline treats the model as an **instrument to be measured**.
It replays the same step to measure how often verdicts flip. It runs tiered
synthetic candidates to check the scoring ranks them correctly. It
preflights prompt changes against the live model, and hands error bars to
the model that reviews results. Our memo pipeline has strong structure (an
outline contract, 46 agents, fact checkers) but grades one run and reports
no variance. Run-to-run replay, live-model preflights and a memo "craft
ledger" are now at the top of our list. They're medium effort, not small.

### The second story: dev harness as product prototype

Their exam-building workflow started as a coding-agent slash command and
became an in-product feature ten days later. Our in-app agent in
augment-it runs on prompts condensed from Claude Code skills. Both silos
prototype product agents inside the coding harness. Neither has a check
that keeps the original and the runtime copy in sync. We at least wrote the
rule down.

## Under the Hood

- **A relay of three subagents** ran the study: evidence, then comparison,
  then the page. A VP-of-Engineering prompt reviewed each before releasing
  the next. The review gates caught a third party's name in the notes, a
  commit count off by one (the agent was right, and our check had the date
  quirk), and a "rule" that should have read "check".
- **The layer split arrived mid-run**, from a human read of the first
  draft. Each step went back to the *same* subagent with the new lens, so
  none of them started over.
- **Diagrams** are [archify](https://github.com/tt-a1i/archify) renders,
  themed with the splash's CSS tokens so they follow the light, dark and
  vibrant modes. The 2×2 is hand-built HTML so it reflows on a phone.
- **Anonymity is checked, not assumed.** The page and its diagram sources
  were searched for every identifier we know of, across the whole built
  site, with zero hits.

## What's Next?

- Share the page with the collaborator, and ask what they'd rather leave
  out.
- On our side: run-to-run replay and live-model preflights for the memo
  pipeline, then a memo craft ledger.
- A pruning pass on our own developer harness, which the comparison showed
  is overbuilt in places.
