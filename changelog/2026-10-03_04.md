## Why Care?

It's easy to compare harnesses and skip the stack underneath them. But the
stack decides how much structure an agent gets for free, and how much
machinery someone has to keep running. So after shipping our
[[2026-10-03_03|silo-to-silo comparison]], we went one level down. We also
did something less comfortable: we took the reasons we give for our own
choices and checked them against what's actually in our repos.

The collaborator's app turned out to use **no front-end framework at all**:
plain JavaScript on Firebase, no build step, no types. Ours is Svelte,
federated apps, Fastify services, a message bus, SurrealDB and a Python
pipeline. Neither is wrong. They're optimized for very different things.

## What's New?

Two new sections on the share page, and four in the study note:

- **The stacks, and what they do to agents.** A side-by-side table, then
  where each stack wins and where it costs.
- **Firestore against SurrealDB**, including the benefits and costs of
  SurrealDB being multi-model.
- **One app against a monorepo of services**, tested against our reason for
  it: less context per agent and fewer overwrites as collaborators scale.
- **Failure isolation**, tested against our other reason: one service
  failing shouldn't take down the rest.

## How it Works

### What held up, and what didn't

| Our reason | What the evidence says |
|---|---|
| Boundaries mean less context per agent task | **Yes**, inside one unit. Work that crosses boundaries gets harder, and every boundary needs its own instructions and checks |
| Boundaries prevent agents overwriting each other | **Partly.** Mostly a git-discipline problem in any shape. The collaborator's worst overwrite came from work done outside git |
| Containers stop one failure taking down the rest | **Only for leaf services.** Every app shares one workspace service, one bus and one database. And our own issue log shows partial failure reads as "a dead click" |
| SurrealDB's multi-model engine | **Mostly unrealized.** We use documents and graph edges. Vector search runs in a separate Chroma store |

### The pattern underneath

**An unusual technology choice gets paid for in harness.** The
collaborator's mainstream choices (Express, Firestore, plain JS) need
almost no explaining to an agent. Ours needed blueprints, skills and
verification passes. Part of why our developer harness is deep is that our
stack is unfamiliar to models.

And the cheapest version of our architecture's benefit isn't
microservices. It's **clear module boundaries with explicit contracts**,
which a modular monolith can have too.

## What's Next?

On our side, the comparison produced concrete work:

- Live/not-live indicators, and timeouts and fallbacks on the socket and
  bus, so partial failure becomes visible instead of a dead click.
- A plan for the shared trunk: redundancy, or a read-only degraded mode.
- Model routing as runtime config, a prerequisite for the replay and
  preflight work we want to borrow.
- Decide on SurrealDB's other models: use them (vector search could retire
  a store) or stop counting them.
