# Three modes, and the palette we actually use

## Why Care?

corpora-builder is the fourth surface in a family of tools built by the same
people for the same work. It did not look like any of them. It shipped navy and
cyan; its siblings render violet and electric magenta on near-black.

That is worth a ship note on its own. What makes it worth *reading* is that the
fix took three attempts, and the first two were confident, well-argued, and
aimed at the wrong layer.

<img src="https://ik.imagekit.io/xvpgfijuw/corpora-builder/labs-theme/Corpora__Labs-Theme--Dark_20260823T033703Z.jpg" alt="The corpora source browser in dark mode: near-black page, monospace chrome, an electric-magenta Sources pill, bordered cards holding source titles in Inter with rows of chips beneath, and one amber-outlined card for a file that would not parse" width="1600" height="840" decoding="async" />

## What's New?

- **Three modes** — light, dark, vibrant — switched from a cycle button in the
  header. Dark is the default.
- **The family palette.** Electric magenta on near-black, from
  `augment-it/packages/theme/theme.css`.
- **The house token architecture**: named palette → theme bindings → consumers,
  with `data-theme` and `data-mode` as independent axes.
- **A monospace instrument panel** wrapped around one reading surface — chrome in
  JetBrains Mono, source titles and excerpts in Inter.
- **`design-drift.mjs`** — five checks, wired into `scripts/check.sh` as a
  blocking rung.

*Added in the 2026-08-23 revision:*

- **A workspace switcher and a one-icon mode toggle** in the header, ported from
  augment-it and the splash pages respectively.
- **An app mark** — a leaning stack of paper — standing where the wordmark was,
  with a different treatment per mode rather than a tint.
- **A theme-aware favicon**, which is the last surface where a mode can still be
  answered at runtime.

## The three attempts

### One: renaming tokens, which changed nothing

The first pass renamed this app's tokens to match augment-it's spelling.
`--ink` → `--color-text`, `--line` → `--color-border`, 39 call sites, no colour
changes. The argument was sound: patterns travel this tree by copy-from rather
than by shared package, so **the vocabulary is the interface**, and a component
cannot be copied across a rename.

It was also invisible. A rename changes no pixels — that is what makes it safe —
and reporting it as convergence was simply wrong. The screenshot that came back
looked identical to the one before, because it was.

### Two: keeping a palette nothing renders

The second pass added borders and density but kept navy/cyan/amber, on the
reasoning that it was the family brand inherited from
`memopop-ai/packages/shared-styles`.

**No shipping app consumes that package.** That fact was already written in this
repo's own token file — memopop-native does not import `shared-styles` despite
its `CLAUDE.md` saying it does; it draws violet out of roughly 700 hardcoded
literals and two locally-reinvented token sets. augment-it draws electric
magenta. Both live surfaces are one violet-magenta family. `shared-styles` is a
fourth palette that nothing paints.

The file documented the drift and then treated the drifted-from package as
authoritative anyway. Twice.

### Three: reading the convention instead of an app

The real error was underneath both. The reference being followed was augment-it's
`DESIGN.md` — which is an *elaboration* of the house system, with federation
vocabulary layered on top. The convention itself lives in
`astro-knots/context-v/blueprints/Maintain-Themes-Mode-Across-CSS-Tailwind.md`
and the `theme-system` skill, and it says three things the elaboration obscures:

| | The convention | What had been built |
|---|---|---|
| Layers | three: named palette → theme bindings → consumers | four tiers, with `--fx-*` as its own |
| Axes | `data-theme` × `data-mode`, independent | one `data-mode`, collapsed |
| Modes | three, and **vibrant is dark-based** | two |

Collapsing the two axes costs nothing until a second brand exists, and then it
costs everything — which is exactly why the theme here is called `labs` and not
`didi`. **Converging the didi.sh brand onto this palette is separate, deliberate
work**, and the axis exists so it does not happen by accident during a restyle.

## Three modes, and vibrant is not light

<img src="https://ik.imagekit.io/xvpgfijuw/corpora-builder/labs-theme/Corpora__Labs-Theme--Light_20260823T033703Z.jpg" alt="The same browser in light mode: warm paper background, deep-violet Sources pill, white cards with visible borders, and the same amber-outlined parse-error card" width="1600" height="1220" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

Light is warm paper and deep violet — not white and grey. The blueprint's
argument for three modes is stakeholder management: nerds pick dark,
traditionalists pick light, design-forward stakeholders pick vibrant, and the
toggle ends the argument before it starts.

<img src="https://ik.imagekit.io/xvpgfijuw/corpora-builder/labs-theme/Corpora__Labs-Theme--Vibrant_20260823T033703Z.jpg" alt="The same browser in vibrant mode: violet-tinted near-black background rather than white, louder magenta pill, and warmer violet card borders, showing that vibrant is dark-based" width="1600" height="1220" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

**Vibrant is dark-based.** The blueprint names the inverse — vibrant inheriting
light's white background — as the classic error, so vibrant gets its own
violet-tinted near-black `void` ramp rather than borrowing dark's greys.

The header cycles rather than toggles. A two-state toggle cannot reach a third
mode, and three modes with no way to reach two of them is dead code.

## Under the hood

### The one deliberate divergence

augment-it is monospace throughout; it operates records. This app is *read* — 845
source titles and excerpts scanned for meaning — and mono costs scanning speed
across a list that long. So chrome is `--font-body` (JetBrains Mono, like the
family) and the reading surface alone is `--font-reading` (Inter).

That is the only exception, it is named in `DESIGN.md`, and the role names are
descriptive rather than `--font-sans` / `--font-mono` per the blueprint's §2.4 —
"heading-1" means nothing to anyone outside the code.

### Rendering found what reading could not

Three careful readings of the CSS missed this. Headless Chrome, against a fixture
page carrying the real `tokens.css` and the real markup, found it in one shot:

```
--color-surface: var(--color__graphite-700);   /* #232634 */
--color-border:  var(--color__graphite-800);   /* #232634 */
```

Identical. Every card drawn as `border: 1px solid var(--color-border)` over
`var(--color-surface)` **painted nothing** in dark mode. It is augment-it's open
gate **A22** — its own contrast pass measures `--color-border` at 1.2–1.5:1
against a 3:1 requirement for control boundaries — and it travelled here with the
palette. `--fx-card-border` now points at `graphite-850`, a darker hairline that
reads as a seam and leaves the border free for hover to move. Light and vibrant
need no override.

The harness costs about a minute and needs no backend. It is now the habit.

### Five checks, and two bugs in the checks

`app/scripts/design-drift.mjs`, zero dependencies, blocking in `check.sh`:

| | |
|---|---|
| **D1** | no component reads a Tier-1 name |
| **D2** | no colour literal below Tier 1 |
| **D3** | a higher step number is never lighter |
| **D4** | `DESIGN.md` agrees with `tokens.css` on every colour Tier 2 uses |
| **D5** | no component reads a token that does not exist |

Each was verified by injecting its violation and confirming it fires. That found
two bugs in the checks themselves:

- **D2** anchored on the string `"Tier 2"` and matched it inside the file's own
  header comment, so on its first run it flagged all 35 primitives.
- **D4** only walked numeric ramp steps, which meant it had never checked the
  accents — the colours a reader of `DESIGN.md` most needs to be right. It now
  covers all 40 named colours Tier 2 points at.

**D3 found two real inversions in the upstream palette**: `paper-200` is lighter
than `paper-100`, and `void-700` is darker than `void-900`. Both are augment-it's
A19 class — a step number that lies is worse than no name, because an agent will
reason from it. They are listed in `KNOWN_UPSTREAM` with the reason rather than
suppressed, so the Tier-1 names stay byte-identical across the two repos.

**D5** is the one worth copying. A `var()` naming nothing paints nothing,
silently, and the CSS is syntactically perfect — augment-it counted 33 of them.

## Added 2026-08-23 — the header, and a mark

Two surfaces the three-mode contract had not reached yet. Neither is its own
ship: they are the same architecture applied twice more, and the interesting part
is where a token swap turned out **not** to be enough.

<img src="https://ik.imagekit.io/xvpgfijuw/corpora-builder/labs-theme/Corpora__Header-with-Mark--Dark_20260823T053355Z.jpg" alt="The corpora window in dark mode: a leaning paper-stack mark where the wordmark was, search and domain filter across the header, a Reach Edu dropdown and moon icon top right, strategy and topic chips grouped by type, and a list of 832 sources" width="1600" height="1154" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

### The workspace stopped being a label

The header read `corpora reach-edu (reach-edu)` — a slug printed twice, with a
bucket name leaking into the product, and no way to see or change which corpus
you were in.

It is now the switcher from `augment-it/shell/src/WorkspaceSwitcher.svelte`, port
rather than dependency: same trigger, same listbox, same interaction contract,
same top-right position so someone who uses both apps does not have to look in
two places. **The trigger carries the display name only**; the slug appears
solely inside the dropdown, where an operator goes when they need to know exactly
what they are pointed at.

Two smaller corrections fell out of it. `display_name` had been defaulting to the
slug — that is where `(reach-edu)` came from — and is now humanised, with
`CORPORA_WORKSPACE_NAME` still winning because no rule turns `ncad-forge` into
*NCAD-Forge*. And `/api/meta` stopped shipping a pre-rendered `label`: a server
that formats a string has made a layout decision on the client's behalf.

### One mode icon that says what comes next

The three glyphs are the canonical ones from
`ai-labs/splash/src/components/ModeToggle.astro`. The splash pages show **all
three** and glow the active one; here it is a straight swap. A three-button group
is right on a marketing page where the modes are part of the pitch — in a dense
tool it is three targets to do one thing.

Which makes the tooltip load-bearing, because an icon that changes on click
without saying what it becomes is a guessing game:

```
Dark mode · click for Vibrant
```

Generated from the cycle rather than written out, and the test asserts that
structurally, so it survives someone reordering the modes.

### The mark

A leaning stack of paper, chosen from ten drafts. The lean is the point — the
brief was *"a pile of paper that's a bit not straightly aligned"*, and an upright
stack is a ream rather than a pile somebody has been working through. It stands
where the wordmark was, with `corpora` kept as the accessible label.

<img src="https://ik.imagekit.io/xvpgfijuw/corpora-builder/labs-theme/Corpora__Mark-Across-Modes_20260823T053355Z.jpg" alt="The same header mark rendered three times: cool paper sheets with a magenta top sheet in dark, full ink sheets on white in light, and glowing magenta sheets in vibrant" width="1600" height="817" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

**Three treatments, not three tints** — and this is where the theme system earned
its keep and also found its edge:

| | sheets | top sheet | glow |
|---|---|---|---|
| dark | cool paper | electric magenta | none |
| light | **full ink** | deep magenta | none |
| vibrant | halo → loud magenta | loud magenta | a real drop-shadow |

Light is a genuine re-cut, not a re-point. **Outline art inverts badly**:
white-on-dark becomes grey-on-white and loses most of its contrast. Everywhere
else in this app a mode is one indirection; here the drawing itself had to change,
which is worth knowing before someone assumes tokens make artwork mode-agnostic.

**Two line counts, one silhouette.** Fifteen edges is right at icon size and mud
in a header — at 26px they land about 1.3px apart and merge into a grey block. The
small cut subsamples the same jittered curve, so outline, lean and top face are
identical and only density changes.

And the icon drafts had a bug that every contact sheet hid: the chosen one
overflowed its own viewBox, so the bottom of the stack was being clipped in every
render shown. Caught by doing the arithmetic while lifting it into a component,
not by looking at it.

### What the packaged icon cannot do

The favicon is now `static/favicon.svg`, carrying **both palettes** and switching
on `prefers-color-scheme` — which an SVG favicon can do and a `.ico` cannot. It
also closes a 404 that had been sitting in the console.

The dock icon gets no such luxury. A `.icns` is bitmaps with no theme awareness,
so it takes one baked palette; three variants are cut and dark is the one to ship.
Worth stating plainly, because "the theme handles it" stops being true at exactly
the boundary where the OS starts drawing instead of the browser.

## What's Next

- **A brand-kit page.** The blueprint expects one; there is nowhere in-app to see
  the palette.
- **Contrast measurement.** augment-it verifies all 108 text-on-surface pairs;
  this repo inherits the numbers without re-measuring them against its own
  surface pairings, and A22 is proof that inheriting is not the same as checking.
- **The didi.sh theme.** The axis is free and the work is not started.
- **Cut the real dock icon** from `mark-dark.svg`. The drafts exist; the `.icns`
  does not.
