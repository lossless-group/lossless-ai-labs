## Why care?

A memo made with the local pipeline existed only on the machine that ran it.
Nobody else could open it, and if it was good, nobody else could build on it.
The connector deployed on 2026-10-10 was meant to fix that, but no existing
memo had moved there, and even a memo written there was final once drafted.

## What changed

- **Existing memos can move to the server.** `scripts/import_firm.py` in the
  orchestrator turns a firm's local folder into the server's shape. Research
  arrives approved, drafts as drafts, and the deck analysis, deal notes and
  finished memo as reference material. Decks, datarooms and exports go to the
  firm's own private bucket. The first client firm is on `memopop.didi.sh` now:
  its finished deals are ready to compile, and the rest pick up where they
  stopped.
- **Colleagues sign in, not swap keys.** A firm's slug on the server is its
  didi.sh organization's slug, so anyone in the organization adds the
  connector in Claude Desktop, signs in, and sees the firm's deals.
- **A finished memo can be revised.** A colleague says what changed (new
  research, a different angle) and Claude reopens just those sections, redoes
  them with the new material, and compiles the next version: `v0.2.3` becomes
  `v0.2.4`. Their own approval of the revised research is enough. A cancelled
  revision puts the memo back as it was.
- **The plugin knows how.** `memopop` plugin 0.2.0 teaches Claude the revise
  flow; re-upload it to pick that up.

The orchestrator's changelog (`2026-10-15_01`) and its `context-v/issues/`
carry the details, including three bugs found and fixed on the way.
