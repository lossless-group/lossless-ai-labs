## Why Care?

The skill said "score inputs, not outcomes" but never said what to do with
the outcome someone actually came for. Told "I want to lose a lot of
weight", it had no move. And it never pushed back on the most common way
these plans fail: starting too many habits at once.

## What's New?

- **`references/goal-pacing.md`**, the philosophy as a method. Process
  goals are leading indicators, scored weekly. Outcomes are lagging
  indicators, paced to milestones at the program form's own Day 30, 60,
  and 90 checkpoints and scored only there, from a Day 0 baseline.
  - Bring an **outcome**: Claude makes it concrete, finds the levers, and
    paces them with real rates (and web research where it matters) so the
    outcome is plausible by day 100, showing the math.
  - Bring a **process goal**: Claude projects a reasonable outcome, as a
    range, split across the Day 30, 60, and 90 milestones.
  - **Layer, don't stack**: start below what feels like enough, add one
    habit or one step of dose at a time, add up the daily load across all
    goals, deload in Weeks 8 and 12.
  - **Safe numbers**: ranges over false precision, conservative health
    rates (sustained fat loss runs roughly 0.5–2 lb a week; early low-carb
    drops are mostly water), doctor sign-off for fasting and hard training.
- **The skill** classifies every goal as outcome or process before drafting
  it, writes the weekly plan as a ramp, checks total load before the closing
  sections, and scores outcomes against their milestones at Day 30, 60,
  and 90.
- **The scored template** gains an outcome line with its Day 0 baseline, a
  pacing section, outcome milestones in the program's By 30 / 60 / 90 Days
  table, and a milestone-vs-actual table.
- **The splash and README** say the same thing in plain words.
