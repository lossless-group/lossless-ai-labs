# Design Brief — Slide 10, Anonymized Founding Circle

> [Build spec — self-contained handoff](#build-spec--self-contained-handoff).
> That section inlines every colour, size, string, and constraint and can be
> pasted whole. The sections before it explain *why*, which is worth reading
> once but isn't needed to execute.

## The problem

Slide 10 currently names fifteen foundations and family offices under the
headline **"You will be in catalytic company."** None of them have committed.
They are pipeline.

That creates three distinct exposures, and they get worse in that order:

1. **It overstates.** A reader scanning a grid of famous names under a headline
   about *company* will read the grid as a roster. It isn't one. If they later
   learn that Rockefeller was a conversation and not a commitment, everything
   else on the slide gets re-read as inflation — including the parts that are
   true.
2. **It burns the prospects.** These families guard their pipeline harder than
   the Foundation guards its own. A named prospect who discovers they were
   listed in a document circulating among their peers has a reason to stop
   taking the call. This is the exposure with a real, non-recoverable cost.
3. **It leaks the strategy.** The list is the go-to-market. It's the most
   valuable page in the deck to a competitor for water philanthropy, and it is
   currently being handed out.

## The insight

The fix is not redaction. Redaction reads as weakness — a grid of grey bars
says *we have nothing*. The fix is that **an unnamed portrait is more
persuasive than a name**, because it makes the reader do the work.

When the page says *"a German industrial dynasty whose initials you'd recognise
on a hood, not a letterhead,"* the reader solves it in about a second and a
half. Having solved it, they own the conclusion. They have also just been
handed a demonstration of exactly how the Foundation will treat *their* name —
which is the actual promise the slide needs to make.

That reframes the whole slide. It stops being a roster and becomes evidence of
discretion. The existing line at the bottom — *"Other notable family offices
and foundations are named on acceptance"* — stops being a footnote and becomes
the thesis. It moves up.

## The hint discipline

Rules the copy must hold to. These are the reusable part of this brief; the
fifteen strings below are just their output.

1. **Never the name, never the product name.** No "BMW," no "IKEA," no
   "Walmart." The hint gestures at the category, the geography, the era, or the
   origin of the fortune.
2. **Solvable in one beat, by the right reader.** The first pitches go to
   family offices, family foundations, and asset managers — principals and
   their advisors, who know this world cold. That audience is what makes the
   whole treatment work: these readers can already name most of the fifteen
   from the city alone, so the hints aren't hiding anything from them. They're
   demonstrating restraint *to* them. A hint that needs Google has failed; a
   hint that reads as a riddle has failed worse. If it takes more than two
   seconds, cut a clause.
3. **Dignified, not clever-clever.** This is an invitation to a founding
   circle, not a pub quiz. Wit is allowed once or twice across the fifteen —
   the Quandt and Minderoo lines carry it. The rest are plain.
4. **Describe the fortune's origin, not the person.** "An iron-ore fortune"
   ages better and offends less than "a mining billionaire." Money has a
   history; people have feelings.
5. **The city stays, verbatim.** Bad Homburg, Bentonville, and Bengaluru are
   half the identification and all of the credibility. Keeping them is also
   what makes the anonymity honest rather than coy — the Foundation isn't
   pretending the reader can't work it out.
6. **Fourteen words maximum, twelve preferred.** Enforced by layout (see
   below), but it's a copy discipline first.
7. **No superlative that can be checked and lost.** "One of the largest" is
   safe; "the largest" invites a correction that costs the whole page.

### The sharpness dial

Hint specificity is a dial, not a fixed setting. The same slide can ship at
three levels, and which one is right depends on who is holding the document.

| Level | Feel | Example (Quandt) | Use when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Categorical** | Safe, generic, low information | "A German industrial family foundation" | The deck goes to a broad list, or anyone downstream may forward it |
| **2 — Portrait** ← *recommended* | Solvable by the informed reader, deniable to everyone else | "A German industrial dynasty whose initials you'd recognise on a hood, not a letterhead" | Named-recipient distribution, one conversation at a time — which is how this deck is described as travelling |
| **3 — Named** | The current slide | "Harald Quandt Foundation" | Only after that specific party has committed and agreed to be named |

**Recommendation: Level 2**, with the copy below. Two consequences worth
accepting deliberately: Level 2 is *designed* to be solvable, so it is not
protection against a determined reader — it is protection against a careless
one, and a statement of intent to the careful one. And if a prospect ever sees
the deck, they should be able to read their own line and find it flattering.
Every string below was written to pass that test. If any of them wouldn't, it
gets cut, not softened.

There is a fourth option worth naming so it can be rejected on purpose:
**drop the roster entirely** and replace it with counts ("six continents,
fifteen conversations, eleven countries"). It is the safest slide and the
weakest one. The specificity is the persuasion; abstraction throws it away.

### Notes on individual lines

- **BENGALURU** is the best line on the page and should not be cut for length.
  A prospect being invited into a *water* founding circle, reading that one of
  the people already in the conversation has funded water and nothing else for
  two decades, is the single most on-thesis sentence in the deck.
- **PERTH** is the longest line at seventeen words and breaks the fourteen-word
  rule. It earns the exception — the marine-ecology doctorate is the detail
  that makes the whole treatment feel considered rather than evasive. If layout
  forces a cut: *"An iron-ore fortune, redirected to the ocean by a magnate with
  a doctorate in marine ecology."*
- **BAD HOMBURG** carries the wit for the European column. Don't add a second
  joke to that column; one is charming, two is a bit.
- **MONACO** is barely anonymised — the city plus "head of state" is a set of
  one. That's acceptable and arguably correct: this is the most public-facing
  of the fifteen and the least likely to object. Flagged so it's a decision,
  not an oversight.
- **MUMBAI** is a corporate foundation rather than a family one. Known
  anomaly, deliberately kept — the hint names the category plainly and doesn't
  pretend otherwise.
- **⚠ ULM** — this hint is deliberately vague because I could not verify the
  origin of the Anton Hieber fortune with confidence, and a wrong attribution
  is worse than a soft one. **Needs the client's line.** Every other hint rests
  on a fact I'm confident about; this one rests on the city.

## Layout consequences

The current row is `Name ·········· CITY` — a two-to-four-word name with the
city right-aligned on the same baseline. Hints run eight to seventeen words.
The existing row structure cannot absorb that; forcing it produces ragged
two-line names with an orphaned city and the alignment collapses.

**Recommended structure — city as eyebrow, hint as body.** Invert the
hierarchy. The city becomes a small-caps eyebrow above the hint, and the hint
wraps as a normal two- or three-line text block.

```
EUROPE ─────────────────────────────

  MONACO
  A reigning head of state's own
  environmental foundation

  BAD HOMBURG
  A German industrial dynasty whose
  initials you'd recognise on a hood,
  not a letterhead
```

Why this and not the alternatives:

- It keeps the **three-column region grid**, which is the slide's best existing
  idea — six continents made visible as geography, not asserted as a claim.
  Fifteen entries across three columns is five, four, and six rows; at two to
  three lines each that is a tall but survivable column on 16:9.
- It makes **the city the scannable element**, which is right. A reader
  skimming should get *Monaco, Bad Homburg, Leiden, Zug, Monheim, Ulm* — the
  geographic spread — and read the hints only where one catches them.
- It **removes the dot-leader problem** entirely. Leaders between a wrapped
  multi-line hint and a right-aligned city are unresolvable.

**Fallback if the columns run long:** drop to two columns and merge Asia and
Oceania into a single "Asia-Pacific" region. Costs one continent's worth of
visible spread; buys roughly forty percent more line length, which lets several
hints collapse to a single line.

**Rejected:** keeping the one-line row and cutting every hint to six words. Six
words yields Level-1 categorical copy — "a German industrial foundation" — and
the slide loses precisely the specificity it exists to convey.

| Column rules | Keep the vertical hairlines between the three regions |

No new tokens. The treatment is entirely a re-composition of existing ones.

## Copy changes elsewhere on the slide

The anonymization changes what the surrounding prose has to do.

**Sub-headline** — currently ends *"...The invitation is being extended quietly,
one conversation at a time, on six continents."* That line now needs to
pre-empt the obvious question ("why isn't anyone named?") before the reader
asks it, so the answer reads as policy rather than evasion. Proposed addition:

> Few families make the connection between safeguarding water, stewardship for
> the generations after them and the geopolitical safety of the places they
> care about. Those who do are the ones we approach. The invitation is being
> extended quietly, one conversation at a time, on six continents — **which is
> why none of them are named here.**

**Closing block** — promote and sharpen. Currently: *"Membership is capped and
personal. Every seat is decided the same way: a conversation, then an
invitation. Other notable family offices and foundations are named on
acceptance."* Proposed:

> Membership is capped and personal. Every seat is decided the same way: a
> conversation, then an invitation. **We name no one until they have said yes —
> including you.**

That last clause is the whole slide. It converts a confidentiality constraint
into a term of membership, and it is the sentence that makes the reader
understand that the discretion on display is a service they are being offered.

**"SIX CONTINENTS" strip** — unchanged. It survives anonymization intact and is
now carrying more of the credibility load, since the geography is the only
fully concrete thing left on the page.

### Canvas

- **1920 × 1080, 16:9, fixed.** A presentation frame, not a responsive page.
- Static composition. No hover states, no animation, no interactivity.
- Side margins **72px**. Top margin **56px**. Footer baseline **56px** from bottom.

### Ground

- Base fill `#042632` (deep blue-green — "water at depth", not slate).
- Over it, one soft radial highlight centred around 35% from left / 30% from
  top: `#0A3D4E` at ~12% opacity, feathered across roughly 70% of the frame.
  Subtle — it should read as depth, not as a spotlight.
- No texture, no imagery, no photographic background on this slide.

### Palette

| Role | Hex | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Ground | `#042632` | Background |
| Foreground | `#fbfffe` | Headline, hint text |
| Accent | `#5BCEFF` | Eyebrow, region headers, "SIX CONTINENTS" label |
| Muted text | `#DEEAE8` | Sub-headline, city eyebrows, closing paragraph |
| Rule / border | `#0369a1` | All hairlines (use at 40–60% opacity) |

**Line-height for all body text: 1.7.** This is a brand rule, not a
suggestion — it is what makes the deck read as unhurried. If the columns don't
fit, cut a hint. Do not tighten leading.

licensed. If neither is available in the design tool, substitute **Nunito Sans**
at 800 for headline weights and 600 for label weights — it holds the humanist
geometry. Second choice **Montserrat**. Do *not* substitute Futura, Poppins, or
Century Gothic; they read colder than this brand.

### The entry row — the one structural change

This is the only thing that differs structurally from the current slide, and
it is the thing most likely to be got wrong.

**Current (wrong for this treatment):**
```
Harald Quandt Foundation ·················· BAD HOMBURG
```
Name left, city right-aligned, one baseline. Cannot absorb a 14-word hint.

**Required:**
```
BAD HOMBURG
A German industrial dynasty whose initials
you'd recognise on a hood, not a letterhead
─────────────────────────────────────────────
```
City becomes a small-caps eyebrow **above** the hint. Hint wraps naturally
beneath at full column width. Hairline separator below each entry.

- Gap between city eyebrow and first hint line: **6px**
- Gap between hint block and the separator beneath: **14px**
- Gap between separator and the next entry's city eyebrow: **18px**
- **No dot leaders anywhere.** They cannot resolve against a wrapped block.
- City is `#DEEAE8`, *not* accent. Accent is reserved for region headers; giving
  cities the same colour flattens the hierarchy into noise.

### Fit check

Column 1 is the tallest at six entries. At 19px/1.7 with most hints wrapping to
two lines, budget ~104px per entry plus ~52px for the region header — roughly
676px of stack against ~560px of available column height below the sub-headline.
**It will overflow.** Resolve in this order:

1. Move `ULM` to the bottom of Column 3 under a merged `EUROPE (cont.)` — ugly,
   last resort.
2. Trim `BAD HOMBURG` to *"A German industrial dynasty you'd recognise on a
   hood, not a letterhead"* and `PERTH` to the short variant noted above.
3. Reduce hint size to 18px — but **not** the 1.7 line-height.
4. Drop to two columns, merging Asia and Oceania into `ASIA-PACIFIC`. Costs a
   continent's worth of visible spread, buys ~40% more line length.

Prefer 2, then 4. Never 3 before 2.

### Do not

- Do not use dot leaders between city and hint.
- Do not name any of the fifteen organisations anywhere on the slide.
- Do not add logos, crests, or wordmarks for the fifteen — that defeats the
  entire treatment in a single stroke.
- Do not tighten line-height below 1.7.
- Do not add a drop shadow to any card or panel; on `#042632` shadows read as
  mud. Depth comes from surface lightness and hairlines.
- Do not introduce a warm or non-blue-green accent.
- Do not make the city eyebrows accent-coloured.

## Open questions for the client

1. **ULM** — what is the Anton Hieber fortune's origin? Needed to write that
   line honestly. (Blocking for that one row only.)
2. **Sharpness level** — is Level 2 right, or does this deck's distribution
   list argue for Level 1? Changes all fifteen strings.
3. **Has any of the fifteen already committed?** A committed party can be
   named, and one real name among fourteen portraits is a *stronger* slide than
   fifteen portraits — it proves the circle is real and demonstrates the
   name-on-acceptance promise in the same glance.
4. **Is fifteen the right number to show at all?** Twelve strong portraits read
   better than fifteen with three weak ones. The pipeline may not need to be
   fully rendered.
