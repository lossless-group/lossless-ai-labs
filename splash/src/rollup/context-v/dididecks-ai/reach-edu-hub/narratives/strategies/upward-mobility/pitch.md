# The Mobility Engine — Pitch Narrative

> Relationship to the CEO deck: Joe's campaign deck is **"what is"** — the model,
> the proof, the $25M ask. [^57ce95] This narrative is **"what's next, what could
> be — with your gift."** Same spine, deeper evidence, and the funder-landscape
> beats the CEO deck deliberately leaves out. Simple slides carry one line each;
> two deliberate "overwhelm" beats (5 and 6) carry the density a PhD program
> officer expects to stress-test.

---

## Beat 1 — The hook (cold open, one line + one face)

> **"Arlie spent twenty years as a library aide. Her job became her degree, her
> degree made her a teacher — and her salary doubled."** [^57ce95]

Twenty years watching the classroom from the aide's desk, a single mom of four,
eighteen months short of a degree she couldn't afford to finish. Then her job
started counting. Two years later she's the teacher of record in the district
she served for two decades — at roughly double her aide salary. [^57ce95]

America still has a mobility engine — the degree. What broke is the on-ramp.
This pitch is about rebuilding it, at scale, with your help.

*(seek chart with Graph/Viz on another pass: Arlie timeline, 2001 → 2024, salary
line doubling at the licensure point)*

## Beat 2 — The problem, in four numbers (simple slide)

> **"Millions of working adults are locked out of upward mobility while jobs
> that require degrees sit empty in their own communities."** [^57ce95]

- **40M** American adults have some college, no degree — placebound in
  entry-level jobs. [^57ce95]
- **4.8M** students walk into school buildings where classrooms lack a certified
  teacher. [^57ce95]
- **1.5M** unfilled openings in teaching and healthcare — good jobs that require
  a degree. [^57ce95]
- **$55K** average loan debt for a new teacher, against $1.8T national student
  debt. [^57ce95]

The campus-based offer to a working adult — *quit, borrow, relocate, maybe
graduate* — filters out exactly the people with the most mobility to gain.
And the last twenty years produced a worse one. The online-program offer —
*borrow, struggle to find time, maybe graduate with debt and a low ROI* —
kept the risk on the working adult and just removed the campus. Two
generations of "access," and in both, the learner holds the downside.

*(seek chart: four-stat tile row; consider a "the offer" vs "who it excludes"
diverging bar)*

## Beat 3 — The insight (the tweet)

> **"What if your job led to your degree — not the other way around?"** [^57ce95]

In every county in America there is a school — and usually a hospital — full of
good jobs that require degrees, staffed by committed adults who don't have one.
Coaches, library aides, paraprofessionals, nursing assistants. They are already
the talent pipeline. They deserve a path up that doesn't require leaving where
they live and work. [^57ce95]

## Beat 4 — The model (simple slide: the ABCs)

> **"The workplace becomes the learning place. The degree comes with a raise,
> not a loan."**

An accredited bachelor's degree defined by its ABCs: [^57ce95]

- **A — Affordability by design.** No candidate pays more than $75/month. Zero
  student debt — engineered, not aspired to.
- **B — Based in the workplace.** From day one to graduation day, candidates
  keep their full-time job, income, and benefits. Employers recruit the students.
- **C — Credit for work.** Roughly half the degree comes from the job itself,
  half from live online tutorials taught by job-embedded Professors of Practice.

Completing the credential from a paraprofessional role is close to a guaranteed
pay bump — Reach's aide-to-teacher-of-record cohorts realize **65–110% wage
gains**. [^57ce95]

> **"Five years in, this isn't a pilot. It's the nation's leading proof point —
> and it beats the national benchmarks that matter."**

|---|---|---|
| Candidates enrolled | 3,400+ across 8 states | — |
| Employer partners | 550 districts & networks | — |
| Retention (post first term) | **93%** | — |
| On-time completion | **70%** | ~50% nationally |
| Wage gain, aide → teacher-of-record | **65–110%** | — |
| Candidate NPS | **60+** | 32 (sector benchmark) |
| Pell-eligible / of color / rural | 65% / 48% / 36% | — |
| Max out-of-pocket | $75/mo, zero debt | $55K avg new-teacher debt |

All Reach internal cohort data and national comparisons per the June 2026
campaign deck. [^57ce95] And the third-party line a program officer will
actually lean on: **Education Analytics measured a ~2.4 effect size on
post-program income in Arkansas** — large enough for statistically valid causal
analysis at today's cohort sizes (80% power) — with **~$20M/yr in projected
aggregate wage gains** for current enrollees. Over 80% of the first Arkansas BA
cohort stepped immediately into full-time teaching at $50,000+ — roughly double
prior wages. Jefferson Parish, LA credits Reach with helping cut teacher
vacancies in half. [^57ce95]

## Beat 6 — The science agrees (OVERWHELM slide #2: the evidence stack)

> **"This model isn't a hypothesis that needs proving. It's the implementation
> of what the evidence already proved."**

- **The degree is the strongest predictor of lasting job quality** — nearly one
  full point (0.90) on the job-quality index vs. no diploma, in the
  Brookings/Child Trends longitudinal study of 3,928 young adults followed to
  age 29. [^679c9b]
- **The relationships at work are the active ingredient.** Work-based learning with a
  supervising adult predicts job quality a decade later (+0.18); job shadowing
  and classroom-only CTE do not. [^679c9b]
- **The timing window is Reach's population.** Training between ages 24–27
  predicts a +0.37 jump in later job quality — the strongest training effect in
  the data. [^679c9b]
- **Earn-and-learn works, per the federal auditor.** GAO: higher pay, little
  student debt, better retention. [^16a15a]
- **The field has certified the model.** The Progressive Policy Institute wrote
  the mobility case; [^a94477] [^9ce917] the Urban Institute's May 2026 scaling
  paper builds on Reach as a lead case; [^7d47c7] Inside Higher Ed names Reach
  the country's only accredited apprenticeship-degree university. [^28ded4]

*(seek chart: evidence "stack" — effect sizes as a horizontal bar family, one
bar per study, sourced)*

## Beat 7 — The ladder is bigger than teaching (the expansion slide)

> **"Wherever a paraprofessional tier sits below a credentialed tier, the
> mobility engine applies. Teachers were the proof — not the limit."**

The same climb — aide today, credentialed professional tomorrow, in place,
debt-free — exists in every shortage field with a working tier below the
license. Reach has already stepped beyond teaching (the Apprenticeship
College of Health [^3f891a]); the demand wall says where the ladder goes
next:

- **Eldercare & direct care — the closest rung.** BLS projects **~765,800
  openings every year** for home health and personal care aides through 2034
  — the largest numeric job growth of any American occupation — at a median
  of just $34,900; [^c94ece] nursing assistants add ~211,800 openings/yr at
  $39,430. [^20ec38] Across the broader direct-care workforce, PHI counts
  **8.9 million total openings 2022–2032**, with median annual earnings of
  only **$25,015** [^fecd96] — while the 65+ population climbs from 61.2M
  toward 82M by 2050. [^ef6763] Five million people are already doing this
  work. The credential ladder out of it does not yet exist. That's Arlie's
  story, waiting to happen in eldercare.
- **Advanced manufacturing.** A projected net need of **3.8M workers
  2024–2033, with roughly half (1.9M) at risk of going unfilled** [^757e16] —
  and an apprenticeship base already 97,500 strong and growing
  20%. [^ee9ca4]
- **Data center operations.** The AI buildout needs people, not just power:
  **46% of operators say they cannot find qualified candidates** (Uptime
  Institute, 2025), [^6777be] against global staffing needs near 2.3M
  roles [^cf95b7] and ~$6.7T of projected global capex by 2030; [^e225f4]
  the U.S. industry already directly employs roughly one million
  people. [^3832d4] These are critical-facility technician, electrical, and
  mechanical roles — and the industry's own staffing research says the
  winning strategy is *removing unnecessary degree requirements* and
  training on the job. [^cf95b7] That is the Apprenticeship Degree's home
  turf.
- **Remote & distributed business administration — the rung for the people
  the labor market can't even see.** One in four American adults lives with
  a disability, [^23cb42] yet only 22.8% of people with disabilities are
  employed versus 65.2% of those without. [^b75cb3] Remote work turned out
  to be an **accessibility technology**: NBER research finds the
  post-pandemic rise in work-from-home explains **68–85% of the record rise
  in full-time employment among workers with physical
  disabilities**, [^272bb5] disabled employment hit a series high in
  2024, [^c49a6d] and disabled workers now telework at a *higher* rate than
  non-disabled workers. [^2e20a0] Of the 21M+ prime-age Americans out of
  the labor force, [^2e54ee] the vast majority cite disability or
  caregiving [^496b0f] — constraints remote work is documented to lower.
  But getting the remote job is not the ladder: in the canonical randomized
  trial, remote workers performed **13% better yet were promoted about half
  as often**, [^c7ebc7] and fully remote U.S. workers were promoted **31%
  less** than office peers in 2023. [^c356ce] Succeeding remotely — the
  tools, the engagement, proof-of-work — and *administering* distributed
  teams is a distinct, teachable, and almost entirely untaught skill set.
  Nobody has built the degree for it.

Full program doctrine — which fields, why those, how to design backward from
the job — lives in [[frontier-job-demand]]. The point for this deck: **upward
mobility isn't limited to teachers and health professionals.** The engine
transfers to wherever the shortage is.

*(seek chart: the ladder grid — four sectors × [paraprofessional wage →
credentialed wage], with annual openings as bubble size)*

## Beat 8 — The moment (the market slide)

> **"Philanthropy just reorganized itself around one outcome: economic
> mobility. This is the purest instrument for buying it."**

The largest new pools in philanthropy are literally chartered for this
narrative's outcome: **NextLadder Ventures — $1B over 15 years for the economic
mobility of low-income Americans** (Ballmer, Gates, Stand Together, Valhalla,
Overdeck); [^5f42a8] **Blue Meridian's $600M+ Place Matters** mobility
portfolio; [^cca778] **MacKenzie Scott's $7.2B year**, [^f868ef] with **$1.1B+ to
access institutions** — HBCUs, tribal colleges, community colleges — while
bypassing elite universities entirely, [^d1d74b] continuing into
2026; [^3c9545] **Lumina's Goal 2040**, re-anchoring national attainment on
credentials of labor-market value. [^6aca82]

And the debt-free degree is now a proven mega-gift category — Ruth Gottesman's
$1B made Einstein tuition-free; [^3bc2ca] Michael Bloomberg's $1B did the same
at Johns Hopkins, explicitly workforce-framed. [^1a4b17] **Both in medicine.
Nobody has written that check for the working adults who are 40-million
strong.**

*(seek chart: funding-flows landscape map — pool size vs. mobility-focus, Reach
positioned in the open quadrant)*

## Beat 9 — The gap is the opportunity (the catalytic slide)

> **"The model is validated. The field is under-capitalized. The flagship bet
> is still open — that's what a catalytic gift buys."**

Mobility philanthropy's capital reaches apprenticeship degrees today as $1–2M
program grants — Carnegie's $2M Delta expansion, [^0a146a] Goodness Web's $1M
behavioral-health pathway. [^7d0be0] Meanwhile the completion-collaborative
pattern (Ballmer/Wilson's $30M in Detroit, [^672e9a] Ascendium's $172M
year [^63bbab]) proves funders will go 8–9 figures for exactly this outcome
when a credible vehicle exists.

Reach is that vehicle, and the campaign math is unusually clean: [^57ce95]

- **$25M over 3 years** carries Reach Teachers College from proof point
  (3,400 enrolled) to **self-sustaining, evaluation-ready scale (7,500+)**.
- That's **~$2,000 per candidate per year** — against an internal cost of
  $8,037/FTE, already lower than WGU ($8,961), SNHU ($11,247), and a fraction
  of the public 4-year average ($52,900).
- At 7,500 enrolled, braided revenue (Pell + $75/mo + employers) funds the
  model **permanently — philanthropy is then no longer required.**
- **$6M is already committed** — Carnegie Corporation ($2M) and Schusterman
  Family Philanthropies ($1M) as anchors, ~$3M more in motion. Commitments by
  August 2026 join the founding growth-partner cohort.

The catalytic frame, stated plainly: **your gift is not charity for a program;
it is the last philanthropic dollar the mobility engine ever needs** — after
which 10,000 working adults turn jobs into degrees by 2030, and the 2035
moonshot (3 million apprenticeship-degree starts across U.S. higher
education [^4aedc1] [^57ce95]) has its proven chassis.

*(seek chart: braided-revenue waterfall → breakeven at 7,500; campaign progress
bar $6M/$25M)*

## Beat 10 — Close

> **"Make the workplace the learning place — and the American Dream reachable
> for all."** [^57ce95]

One person's climb is a two-generation event. Forty million people are waiting
at the on-ramp. The engine works, the evidence is in, the anchors are in the
boat. What's next is scale — and scale is what your gift catalyzes.
