# Foundational Capabilities, Measured Improvements — Pitch Narrative

> CEO deck = "what is." [^57ce95] This arc = a focused, grant-sized "what's
> next": fund the foundational-skills layer *inside* a degree that already
> works. The door for this audience is **measurement** — adult-literacy funders
> have spent decades funding programs whose gains were assumed, never measured.
> The vision in one line: *what K-12 learned, delivered in the moments working
> adults actually have, measured as it goes, shared with the movement, and
> compounding into the next generation.*

---

## Beat 1 — The hook

> **"The labor market pays for the skill, not the diploma."**

The cleanest evidence in labor economics says literacy and numeracy
independently predict earnings — *after* controlling for years of schooling,
credentials, experience, and demographics. Hanushek and colleagues, across 23
countries of PIAAC data: a one-standard-deviation gain in numeracy is
associated with **~18% higher wages for prime-age workers on average — and
28% in the United States, the highest return in the study**. [^28f12b] [^2b7e6d]
America has both the biggest skills penalty and the biggest skills payoff.

## Beat 2 — Who's below the line (simple slide, hard numbers)

> **"Most American working adults are below proficiency in exactly the skills
> the market prices highest."**

- **28% of U.S. adults — more than 1 in 4 — now read at Level 1 or below, up
  from 19% in 2017.** [^24ff0f] [^a182e2] The trend is the headline: nine
  points of decline in six years.
- Only **44%** of U.S. adults reached Level 3+ literacy in PIAAC 2023, and
  just **38%** in numeracy; average scores declined significantly from
  2017. [^24ff0f] [^d67dcb]
- In the frontline service-sector workforce (retail, food service, health &
  social assistance) — the Apprenticeship Degree's core population — a
  National Skills Coalition analysis of PIAAC data (with AIR) found **62%
  score below Level 3 in literacy and 74% below Level 3 in
  numeracy**. [^a73ccd] [^d461ae]

*(seek chart: proficiency distribution with the US premium line overlaid — "the
skills that pay most are the skills most lack")*

## Beat 3 — The uncomfortable truth about credentials (the turn)

> **"K-12 spent twenty years learning what works and how to measure it.
> Postsecondary never tried — because the degree assumes."**

We are not short on knowledge. K-12 rebuilt reading instruction around the
science of reading — evidence-based practice, and just as importantly,
**relentless measurement**: screeners, benchmarks, growth tracking, public
accountability. [^cd9feb] Higher education adopted none of it, because higher
education runs on an assumption: that the basics arrived before the student
did. For a first-generation working adult twenty years out of a
under-resourced high school, that assumption is the quiet failure mode of
adult credentialing — the diploma arrives, the underlying reading and math may
not have moved, and the wage premium the skill would have earned never fully
materializes. No one notices, because no one measured.

And the one thing postsecondary *does* try — remediation — is **diversion,
not development**: the rigorous evidence finds pull-out developmental courses
do little to build skills, and their primary effect is diverting students
away from college-level work. [^f2068b] A CUNY randomized trial made the
counterpoint concrete: students placed directly into college-level statistics
with support passed at a **16-point higher rate** than those sent to remedial
algebra, and went on to earn more credits. [^4a210e] The alternative the
evidence favors — **applied, relevant, contextual practice** — has heretofore
not really been possible to deliver to working adults at scale. **We think we
can create a breakthrough.** The next two beats are how.

## Beat 4 — The pedagogy finally caught up (the good news)

> **"The science of reading rebuilt children's literacy. It's now reaching
> adults — and the delivery vehicle it needs already exists."**

The validated evidence base that reshaped K-12 literacy is being adapted for
adult learners, [^cd9feb] and IES's adult-education evidence reviews catalog
contextualized, work-embedded numeracy strategies that outperform
decontextualized drill. [^95f776] The missing piece was always the vehicle: a
place where working adults are *already* enrolled, persisting, and applying
skills daily. The Apprenticeship Degree teaches in the flow of work by
design [^57ce95] — the natural home for contextualized foundational-skills
instruction.

## Beat 5 — The recipe (the delivery slide)

> **"Woven into the work, aimed at the raise, sized for the moments they
> actually have — and measured as it goes."**

The recipe for foundational skills that actually sticks for working adults:

- **Contextually relevant.** The reading is the curriculum guide, the parent
  email, the medication chart; the math is the classroom count, the dosage,
  the paycheck. Skills taught inside the work they already do — the approach
  the evidence favors over decontextualized drill. [^95f776]
- **Consequential.** Every gain advances a real credential toward a real
  raise — 65–110% at licensure [^57ce95] — so motivation never has to be
  manufactured.
- **Interstitial.** Working adults don't have semesters; they have shift
  breaks, commutes, and the small in-between moments. Instruction sized for
  those interstitial moments is instruction that actually happens.
- **Continuously measured.** And here is the quiet superpower: **ongoing
  mobile instruction produces ongoing measurement.** When learning happens in
  small daily doses, assessment stops being a sit-down event and becomes
  exhaust from the instruction itself — a growth curve per candidate, per
  cohort, in real time.

**Audio-based AI is what makes this buildable now.** A spoken micro-lesson on
the commute; a voice check-in after a shift; fluency, comprehension, and
number sense evaluated from natural speech. What used to be difficult and
blunt — paper assessments, pull-out classes, annual testing windows — becomes
**API-implementable and precise**. (This is the same voice-first thesis as the
[[agentic-product-lab]] NCAD Forge, applied to foundational skills — the two
strategies share an engineering spine.)

## Beat 6 — The vehicle's own numbers (proof slide)

> **"We're not proposing a literacy program that needs students. We have 3,400
> — with 93% retention."** [^57ce95]

3,400+ candidates across 8 states · 65% Pell-eligible, 48% candidates of
color, 36% rural · **93% retention** post first term · **70% on-time
completion** vs ~50% nationally · $75/month maximum, zero debt · wage gains of
**65–110%** at licensure. [^57ce95] Add a measured foundational-skills layer to
this machine and every point of literacy/numeracy gain compounds into the wage
outcome the evidence promises. [^28f12b]

## Beat 7 — The funding landscape (OVERWHELM: the field slide)

> **"Adult literacy is philanthropy's most under-capitalized lane — which
> makes measured improvement the differentiator worth funding."**

The 2023 → mid-2026 scan of the lane:

- The anchor funder, **Dollar General Literacy Foundation**, is escalating —
  record one-day adult/family literacy rounds of $10.6M (2024) → $13.2M (2025)
  → ~$16M (2026), including GED/HSE support at colleges. [^79319a] [^90ad13] [^3f6fb6]
- The **Barbara Bush Fund for Family Literacy** relaunched (Nov 2025) as a
  national grantmaking fund — two-generation family literacy, exactly the
  parent-learner demographic Reach serves. [^69fa84]
- **MacKenzie Scott's Open Call** put $1–2M unrestricted into community
  adult-literacy providers — the largest infusion those organizations have
  ever seen. [^b8a0cd]
- Meanwhile the typical adult-literacy grant is four figures — the field's
  own re-granting fund caps at $6,000 per grant, backfilling a federal
  collapse from ~$1B (2005) to ~$190M (2024). No scalable, measured flagship
  exists. That's the opening. [^e174dc]

*(seek chart: lane-size comparison — adult literacy vs adjacent lanes, with
the DGLF escalation inset)*

## Beat 8 — What your gift catalyzes (the ask)

> **"Fund measured improvements: cognitive and foundational skills woven
> into the work, growing as they are measured, visible at graduation — and
> shared with the Apprenticeship Degree movement."**

The grant shape (program-scale, not campaign-scale): fund the adaptation of
science-of-reading and contextualized-numeracy curricula into Reach's tutorial
sequence, [^cd9feb] [^95f776] the audio-AI instruction-and-assessment layer
that fits candidates' interstitial moments, and public, PIAAC-aligned
reporting of skill-gain per cohort. The deliverable is the thing this field
has never had: **an auditable, per-cohort answer to "did the basics actually
improve?"** — attached to a population of thousands, retained at 93%, whose
wage outcomes are already tracked. [^57ce95]

And the result doesn't stay at Reach. **What works — the curriculum
adaptations, the measurement instruments, and wherever possible the delivery
technology itself — gets shared with the wider NCAD community**, [^4aedc1] so
every member institution serving working adults inherits the playbook. One
grant, one proof, a movement's worth of adoption.

Catalytic frame: a modest grant here doesn't run a literacy program — it makes
Reach the **first university whose foundational-skills improvements are
measured and real** — and hands the adult-literacy field its
proof-of-improvement flagship.

## Beat 9 — The two-generation dividend (the payoff slide)

> **"Teach a parent to read better, and you've made two investments. Literacy
> is statistically inter-generational."**

Parents' literacy and numeracy spill over into their children's outcomes —
the statistical backbone of the entire family-literacy movement, and the
reason the Barbara Bush Fund exists in its current form. [^69fa84] An
NIH-funded study of 3,000+ families found **a mother's reading skill was the
single strongest predictor of her children's later academic success — ahead
of neighborhood and family income**; [^4872f7] and OECD analysis shows
parental background predicts adult skills more strongly in the U.S. than in
almost any other surveyed country. [^6a9ca0] Now look at
who Reach's candidates are: parents, working as paraprofessionals in the
schools their own children attend. [^57ce95] A measured literacy gain for
this population lands twice — once in the candidate's paycheck, once at their
kitchen table — which makes this strategy a direct input to the
[[upward-mobility]] thesis: one adult's climb is a two-generation event.

## Beat 10 — Close

> **"Millions completed programs. Fewer gained the skills. We'll weave the
> skills into the work, measure the improvement — and watch it show up in
> their children."**
