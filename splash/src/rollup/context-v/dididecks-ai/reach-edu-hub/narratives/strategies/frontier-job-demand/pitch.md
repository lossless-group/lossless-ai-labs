# The Job Is the Curriculum — Pitch Narrative

> CEO deck = "what is": the teaching proof point, and the explicit "where this
> is going" — pathways beyond teaching into healthcare and advanced
> manufacturing. [^57ce95] This arc = "what's next": which frontier fields, why
> those, and what a gift launches. The door for this audience is **demand** —
> they see the shortage numbers weekly and doubt higher ed can move fast enough.

---

## Beat 1 — The hook

> **"The course catalog is a lagging indicator. In frontier fields, the job is
> the curriculum."**

Robotics lines, drone operations, applied AI — these fields reinvent
themselves faster than any curriculum committee cycle. A campus can't keep up.
A workplace can't fall behind. So put the degree where the work is.

## Beat 2 — The model already proved it transfers (proof slide)

> **"The ABCs took an aide to teacher-of-record with a 65–110% raise. The same
> ABCs took a nursing assistant into healthcare. Frontier fields are next."**

The Apprenticeship Degree's structure is field-agnostic: affordability by
design ($75/month, zero debt), based in the workplace, credit for
work. [^57ce95] Five years of teaching outcomes — 93% retention, 70% on-time
completion, a ~2.4 measured effect size on income [^57ce95] — plus the
Apprenticeship College of Health launch prove the chassis carries new
payloads. [^3f891a] And apprenticeship's own outcomes in technical fields
already lead the pack: per the U.S. Department of Labor, **93% of apprentices
who complete a Registered Apprenticeship retain employment, with an average
annual salary of $77,000**. [^74d051]

## Beat 3 — The three opinionated bets (simple slide)

> **"We don't offer everything. We design backward from three frontier fields
> where demand, employer pull, and apprenticeship fit all score high."**

1. **Advanced Manufacturing Operations** — software-driven lines, robotics,
   observability.
2. **Drone Sciences** — manufacture, software, deployment, applications.
3. **AI Technology in Business Operations** — making AI actually deliver
   inside organizations.

A fourth candidate has forced its way onto the list: **Data Center
Operations** — the physical-infrastructure jobs of the AI buildout. And a
fifth with a different shape: **Remote & Distributed Business
Administration** — a job category the catalog never built at all (both on
the demand wall below).

Opinion is the point: a program for everyone serves no employer well.

## Beat 4 — The demand wall (OVERWHELM #1: the numbers)

> **"Every one of these fields is short by six or seven figures of people — and
> employers are already co-funding the training."**

**Advanced manufacturing:** Deloitte and The Manufacturing Institute (2024)
project a net need of **~3.8M new manufacturing employees between 2024 and
2033 — and roughly half of them (1.9M) could go unfilled** if the skills and
applicant gaps aren't addressed; [^757e16] over 65% of manufacturers name
attracting and retaining talent their primary business challenge. [^757e16]
Already an apprenticeship stronghold — **97,500 registered apprentices served
in 2025, +20% over five years**, [^ee9ca4] with JFF alone providing technical
assistance to **800+ employers** building or expanding programs
(2020–2025). [^405c2d]

**Drone sciences:** the global drone market is projected to grow from **~$73B
(2024) to ~$163.6B by 2030** at a 14.3% CAGR, with the U.S. the largest
single market; [^7bb99e] PwC UK projects **628,000 jobs in the UK drone
economy alone by 2030** [^69bdec] — a scale signal for the larger U.S. market.

**AI in business operations:** roughly nine in ten organizations now use AI
somewhere in operations (McKinsey's State of AI), [^42684f] yet Gartner finds
only **9% have reached AI maturity** — deploying AI widely across business
units [^da5b04] — an enormous operationalization gap. BCG's microeconomic
model projects **50–55% of U.S. jobs reshaped by AI within 2–3 years** —
reshaped, not replaced; [^c1cc24] PwC's 2026 Global AI Jobs Barometer (1B+
job ads) finds AI-professionalised jobs growing **2× faster** than
AI-democratised jobs, with **42% faster wage growth** since 2021. [^43d7ac]

**Data center operations (the fourth candidate):** the AI buildout needs
people, not just power — **46% of operators report they cannot find
qualified candidates**, and 37% struggle to retain staff (Uptime Institute
2025 global survey); [^6777be] global staffing needs approach **2.3M
full-time roles** across 230+ specialist jobs; [^cf95b7] McKinsey projects
**~$6.7T of global data-center capex by 2030**; [^e225f4] the U.S. industry
already directly employs roughly **one million people**. [^3832d4]
Critical-facility technician, electrical, and mechanical roles typically
require no four-year degree — and Uptime's own staffing research says the
winning strategy is *removing unnecessary education requirements and
training on the job*. [^cf95b7] A field begging for an apprenticeship
degree. (Framing discipline: cite the ~1M *direct* jobs, not the
industry-commissioned "5.5M supported" multiplier; label Uptime/McKinsey
figures as global.)

**Remote & distributed business administration (the fifth candidate — a
different shape):** here the frontier isn't a technology outrunning the
catalog; it's a work mode the catalog **never taught at all**. Roughly
**25% of all U.S. paid workdays are now worked from home — 3.5× the 2019
level, and stable**; [^a057d7] remote roles have drawn **46% of LinkedIn
applications from ~10% of postings** [^44bfd1] — a large, durable, and
ferociously contested market. The population-side signal is even stronger:
remote work is the documented accessibility technology behind record
disabled employment (NBER: WFH explains 68–85% of the rise in full-time
disability employment), [^272bb5] [^c49a6d] and the majority of the 21M+
prime-age non-participants cite exactly the constraints — disability,
caregiving — that remote work lowers. [^2e54ee] [^496b0f] Yet the ladder is
broken: remote workers in the canonical RCT performed 13% better but were
promoted **~half as often**, [^c7ebc7] and fully remote U.S. workers were
promoted **31% less** in 2023. [^c356ce] Engagement, tool fluency,
proof-of-work, and *managing teams you never meet* are distinct, teachable
competencies with no degree pathway anywhere. Design one backward from the
distributed workplace, and the employer pull is every company that went
remote and never learned to run it.

All figures verified against primary sources 2026-07-28 (corrections: the
old "2.1M by 2030" was the superseded 2021 study; "94% skills gap" was a
misread partnership stat; the "$163.6B" is global, not U.S.; the "78% drone
integration (PwC)" stat traced to no real PwC publication and was dropped).
Hero stats: **3.8M/1.9M + 97,500 apprentices** for manufacturing; **PwC
Barometer + BCG reshaped-not-replaced** for AI; the drone hero is the global
market curve, honestly labeled.

## Beat 5 — The money already voted (OVERWHELM #2: the funding wave)

> **"In 2026, corporate philanthropy went nine-figure on exactly these fields.
> None of it yet buys a degree."**

BlackRock's $100M Future Builders (trades, with a $25M RFP run by
JFF); [^a25592] Lowe's five-fold expansion to $250M for 250,000
tradespeople; [^e33e8d] Meta's $115M America's Workforce Academy, explicitly
tied to data-center-adjacent skilled jobs; [^e9c685] Bloomberg's $90M National
Skilled Trades Initiative atop $570M cumulative CTE; [^65140c] Microsoft's $4B
Elevate targeting 20M AI credentials; [^2d173e] Google's $1B for AI training
across accredited nonprofit colleges — Reach is directly
eligible; [^611b39] Google.org's $75M AI Opportunity Fund for essential AI
skills. [^29691f]

The pattern: certificates, boot camps, academies — **training without the
degree**. The evidence says the degree is what compounds (it's the strongest
single predictor of lasting job quality [^679c9b]). The open position in this
wave is the accredited, debt-free, work-embedded degree — and Reach is the
only institution accredited to deliver it. [^28ded4]

*(seek chart: the wave as stacked commitments timeline 2023→2026, with a
"degree-bearing?" flag per commitment — all No except Reach)*

## Beat 6 — What your gift catalyzes (the ask)

> **"Fund the program design, and the employers fund the students."**

The frontier playbook per field: co-design backward from a hiring employer's
role (competencies → curriculum → credit mapping), stand up the first cohort
with NCAD-network employer partners, [^4aedc1] and hold the quality bar that
made teaching work (150:1 advising, job-embedded faculty [^57ce95]). Because
candidates are employed from day one and braided revenue covers delivery at
scale, [^57ce95] a philanthropic gift here is pure **program-launch capital**:
it buys the design sprints, faculty leads, and accreditation pathway for the
first apprenticeship degrees in advanced manufacturing, drone sciences, and
applied AI — categories no one else can accredit today.

Catalytic frame: the teaching college took five years to become the national
proof point. [^57ce95] Each new frontier program starts with that chassis,
those systems, and a funding wave already paying for everything around it.

## Beat 7 — Close

> **"In fields this new and moving this fast, there is no chance of academic
> program design keeping up. The only hope is apprenticeship — on-the-job
> skill development, combined with verified learning and broader industry
> knowledge."**
