# Fish Where the Whales Are — Pitch Narrative (Internal)

> Concentrate development effort on the few funders who can write the biggest aligned checks, and design each ask to their pattern at maximum plausible size.

---

## Beat 1 — The hook

> **"Most fundraising is done fishing with a wide net in a whale ocean."**
> Fundraising effort is spread widely. Money isn't. Funding obeys a power law, and the biggest checks come from a few hundred hands.

## Beat 2 — The shape of the money (the numbers slide)

> **"$617 billion a year, concentrated in a tenth of a percent of hands —
> with a $124 trillion wave behind it."**

- **The pool is at a record.** U.S. charitable giving hit **$617.2B in 2025**
  — crossing $600B for the first time (+5.7% current dollars, +3.0% after
  inflation). [^4c940e]
- **The concentration is radical.** Of ~120,000 U.S. foundations, grantmakers
  giving $50M+ are just **0.1% of funders — yet they supply 40% of all grant
  dollars**; [^6fced1] the largest 1,000 (~1% of funders) account for roughly
  half to 60% of tracked foundation giving. [^b3229d] The Gates Foundation —
  the largest U.S. private foundation — alone paid out **$8.0B in
  2024**; [^1a2cb7] marquee grantmakers like Mellon (~$540M in 2024
  grants) [^f4fc11] and Robert Wood Johnson (~$550M/yr) [^5c3e27] each move
  over half a billion annually.
- **The wave got bigger.** Cerulli now projects **$124T changing hands
  through 2048** in the Great Wealth Transfer — with **~$18T destined for
  charity**. [^f4987b]
- **The whales are multiplying.** 556,850 ultra-high-net-worth individuals
  ($30M+) held **$63.8T globally in 2025**, and the U.S. is home to ~37% of
  them — roughly 206,000 people; [^552e9e] global HNWI wealth reached a
  record $98.3T (Capgemini). [^56142e] World figures — labeled as such on
  the slide.

All figures verified against primary sources 2026-07-28. Corrections from
verification: the June draft's Mellon $302M / RWJ $293M were ~2015-era
numbers on an undated aggregator page — the single most dangerous figures in
the deck for a foundation-literate audience; Giving USA, Cerulli, and Altrata
all had newer editions than the draft cited; and the $98.3T belongs to
Capgemini's investable-assets series, not Altrata's net-worth counts (the two
must never be mixed).

## Beat 3 — The method (the doctrine slide)

> **"The 990 tells you what they funded. The call tells you why. The pattern
> tells you what to ask for — and how big."**

1. **Study the public footprint** — 990s for actual grantmaking
   history, [^a7d443] Candid/press/principals' statements for strategy and
   values. [^4b538e]
2. **Get the intro call** — learn the thesis behind the checks.
3. **Pattern-recognize** — the cause, geography, stage, and outcome they keep
   paying for.
4. **Align to the sweet spot** — design the ask to the *largest amount that
   still fits their pattern*. Start from what they want to give; shape the
   program to match.

## Beat 4 — It already worked (the receipts slide)

> **"The campaign's anchors are reverse-engineering receipts: Carnegie's
> pattern said Delta, so the ask said Delta — and $2M landed."**

Carnegie Corporation's $2M anchors the Delta expansion; Schusterman's $1M
followed; $6M of $25M is committed with ~$3M more in motion and an August 2026
founding-cohort close. [^57ce95] [^0a146a] Each landed where the funder's
existing pattern and Reach's work overlap — not from a generic application.

## Beat 5 — The whale map is already drawn (OVERWHELM: the target slide)

> **"The mega-gifts research is a reverse-engineered map: 84 verified gifts,
> every funder's revealed pattern, sized and dated."**

What the map shows, by tier:

- **Pattern-proven, in-market now:** Ballmer Group (largest private funder of
  alternative teacher prep; $40M residency renewal) [^e7cbef] · Ascendium ($172M/yr,
  rural learner success named) [^63bbab] · Bloomberg ($570M cumulative CTE,
  now granting directly to colleges) [^65140c] [^a26016] · Walton (Strategy 2030
  aimed at the Delta) [^5e224c] · Arnold Ventures (largest-ever Maricopa
  completion grant) [^a26016] · Lumina (Goal 2040 metric = Reach's
  outcomes). [^6aca82]
- **Dated windows:** Humanity AI $10M open call (summer 2026); [^08f3c1]
  OpenAI $50M unrestricted (July 2026); [^1b000c] BlackRock's $25M RFP via
  JFF. [^a25592]
- **Unsolicitable but screen-matched:** MacKenzie Scott — $7.2B year, [^f868ef]
  $1.1B+ to access institutions exactly like Reach [^d1d74b] — the play is
  public legibility and clean outcomes data, not an application.
- **The whale thesis in one line:** billion-dollar debt-free-degree checks
  exist (Gottesman, [^3bc2ca] Bloomberg/Hopkins [^1a4b17]) — **but only in
  medicine.** The pattern-matched mega-ask for undergraduate working adults
  has never been made. Someone will make it first.

*(seek chart: capacity × alignment scatter — every scanned funder plotted,
top-right quadrant labeled "spend the hours here")*

## Beat 6 — The engine (the operationalization slide)

> **"Reverse-engineering is the strategy. The corpus + agents are the engine."**

The reach-edu funder corpus (500+ documents across 57 funders, now with
per-strategy funding datasets and a 150-source citation registry) plus the
[[agent-workflow-maxxing]] doctrine make studying many funders' footprints
tractable for a small team. The mega-gifts scan — 84 verified gifts in an
afternoon — is the throughput proof.

## Beat 7 — The ask (internal decision)

> **"Rank every prospect by capacity × alignment. Spend the team's hours on
> the top ten. Size every ask to the pattern's maximum."**

Concretely: adopt the tiered map from Beat 5 as the working pipeline; assign
the intro-call push to the pattern-proven tier before the August cohort
close; [^57ce95] submit into every dated window that clears the alignment bar;
and draft the first pattern-matched mega-ask (the medicine-precedent,
debt-free-degree framing) for the one funder whose pattern it fits best.

## Beat 8 — Close

> **"A few precise asks beat a hundred generic ones. The map is drawn — now
> fish where the whales are."**
