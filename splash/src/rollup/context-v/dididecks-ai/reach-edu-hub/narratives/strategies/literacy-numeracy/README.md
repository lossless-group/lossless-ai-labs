## The through-line

There is a quiet ceiling on adult economic mobility: **foundational skills**.
Decades of evidence — most cleanly the OECD's PIAAC survey of adult
skills [^d67dcb] — show that **literacy and numeracy independently predict
earnings, employment, and advancement**, *even after controlling* for years of
schooling, credentials, experience, and demographics. [^28f12b] [^2b7e6d] The
skill itself is paid for, not just the diploma.

But two facts collide. First, a large share of working adults sit **below
proficiency** in exactly these skills [^24ff0f] — concentrated in the frontline
and service-sector workers who are the Apprenticeship Degree's core
population. [^d461ae] Second, a degree program can hand someone a **credential
without guaranteeing the underlying basics actually improved** — capability and
certification come apart.

The strategy closes that gap. The pedagogy is finally ready: the **science of
reading** (the evidence base that reshaped K-12 literacy) is now reaching adult
education, [^cd9feb] and **contextualized, embedded numeracy** instruction has a
growing evidence base. [^95f776] Fold those into the Apprenticeship Degree —
taught *in the flow of work*, contextualized to the job — and **measure the
gain** (PIAAC-style pre/post). The result: the degree doesn't just certify; it
durably lifts the literacy and numeracy the labor market rewards, locking in
the income and promotion gains and making them real rather than nominal.

So the reframe for a funder: this is not remediation tacked onto a degree. It is
**measured improvement** — an Apprenticeship Degree graduate leaves
measurably stronger, demonstrably, in the two skills most tightly linked to
lifetime earnings.

## Why it moves income — the mechanism

1. **The skills are independently priced.** PIAAC wage analyses find a sizable
   earnings premium per standard deviation of numeracy/literacy, robust to
   controls for education. Verified against the paper (2026-07-28): Hanushek
   et al. (2015) report a **~18% wage premium per SD of numeracy for
   prime-age workers across 23 countries — and 28% in the U.S., the highest
   return in the study**. [^28f12b] [^2b7e6d] (Framing note: it's an earnings
   gradient among full-time workers, not a causal rate of return — the
   authors say so explicitly.)
2. **Promotions, not just starting wage.** Higher foundational skills track with
   advancement and on-the-job learning over the career, so the gain compounds —
   the lifecycle return is larger than early-career snapshots suggest. [^2b7e6d]
   ⚠ VERIFY.
3. **Capability is durable.** Unlike a one-time credential signal, a real gain in
   reading/math travels with the worker across roles and employers.
4. **It de-risks the degree's promise.** Measured gains turn "you earned a
   degree" into "you can demonstrably do more" — which is what sustains the raise.

## The gap this fills

- **Most working adults are below proficiency — and it's getting worse.**
  Verified (2026-07-28): PIAAC 2023 (U.S.) — only 44% of adults reached
  Level 3+ in literacy and 38% in numeracy, and **28% now read at Level 1 or
  below, up from 19% in 2017** — a nine-point decline in six
  years. [^24ff0f] [^a182e2] [^d67dcb] (The 2017-era "19% / 43M adults"
  figure is stale as a standalone stat; use it only as the trend baseline.)
- **The Apprenticeship Degree's population is the most affected.** Verified
  (2026-07-28): a National Skills Coalition analysis of PIAAC data (with
  AIR's PIAAC team) found **62% of frontline service-sector workers**
  (retail, food service, health & social assistance) **below Level 3 in
  literacy and 74% below Level 3 in numeracy** — precisely the frontline
  workers Reach serves. [^a73ccd] [^d461ae] (Say "below Level 3," not
  "limited," to a PIAAC-literate audience.)
- **Degrees can certify without lifting the basics.** Without explicit,
  measured foundational-skills instruction, the gain is assumed, never measured.

## The pedagogy is ready

- **Science of reading → adult education.** The validated evidence base that
  reshaped children's literacy (phonological awareness, decoding, fluency,
  vocabulary, comprehension as connected processes) is now being adapted for
  adult learners (e.g. ProLiteracy's work on the science of reading in adult
  education). [^cd9feb] ⚠ VERIFY specifics.
- **Contextualized / embedded numeracy.** Teaching math *in the context of the
  work and life tasks adults actually do* outperforms decontextualized drill;
  IES adult-education evidence reviews catalog promising
  strategies. [^95f776] ⚠ VERIFY.
- **Work-embedded delivery fits the model.** The Apprenticeship Degree already
  teaches in the flow of work — the natural vehicle for contextualized
  foundational-skills instruction.

## Measure the improvement

The differentiator is **measured improvement**: pre/post assessment (PIAAC-aligned or a
validated instrument) so every cohort's literacy/numeracy gain is *visible*. That
turns the strategy into a fundable, evaluable program with a clear outcome metric
(skill-level movement) sitting upstream of the mobility metric (wage/promotion).
