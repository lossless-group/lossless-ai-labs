# The Raise That Stays — Pitch Narrative

> The door for this audience is **place**: a rural funder has
> watched talent, wages, and institutions drain out of their geography for
> decades. Lead with the loop, land on the Delta.

---

## Beat 1 — The hook

> **"Rural America doesn't have a talent shortage. It has an exit problem —
> every traditional credential is a bus ticket out."**

The loop is self-reinforcing: chronic workforce shortages (teachers first,
then healthcare), no affordable local path to a degree, and the steady
out-migration of anyone who earns one — exporting both the talent and the
income that talent would have spent locally. [^542c7f] [^1032a6]

## Beat 2 — The map (simple slide)

> **"In America's education deserts there is no college within commuting
> distance. There is always a school."**

Reach grows fastest in exactly the counties traditional higher ed never
reached — the education deserts with zero or one broad-access college in
commuting range. [^57ce95] In every one of those counties sits a school full of
degree-requiring jobs and committed adults that lack degrees — aides,
paraprofessionals, coaches, secretaries -- already working. [^57ce95] 36% of Reach's candidates
are rural. [^57ce95]

*(seek chart: the campaign deck's twin maps — "where we reach" vs education
deserts — overlaid rather than side-by-side)*

## Beat 3 — The mechanism (the tweet)

> **"Start with a job, end with a better one — without borrowing _or relocating_."**
> **"The raise is the point, and the raise stays."**

The apprentice is a paid employee throughout — GAO: higher pay, little debt,
better retention. [^16a15a] The credential is debt-free, so the wage jump at
licensure is *kept*, not clawed back by loan payments — worth more where
baseline incomes are lower. [^a94477] And because the workplace is the campus,
nobody relocates to a college town: the credential, the raise, and the
household spending all land in the same county. Grow-your-own completers stay
— districts "no longer need job fairs." [^a6412b]

And the engine has a second gear the school and hospital don't cap: **remote
& distributed business administration** — income that lands in the county
*without even requiring a local employer*. Rural America works from home at
half the metro rate (8.1% vs 16.2%) [^aabd55] — not for lack of demand
(~25% of all U.S. paid workdays are now remote [^a057d7]) but for lack of
the on-ramp; economic developers already treat remote work as a rural
strategy for exactly this reason. [^ce4b4f] [^91c42b] Teach rural working
adults to succeed in — and then *administer* — distributed teams, and the
metro wage arrives without the moving truck. (Full program case:
[[frontier-job-demand]], fifth candidate; [[upward-mobility]], Beat 7.)

## Beat 4 — Arkansas already proved it (the proof slide)

> **"This isn't a rural hypothesis. It's an Arkansas result: a ~2.4 effect
> size on income."**

Education Analytics found an exceptionally large effect on post-program income
in Arkansas — statistically valid causal analysis is possible at today's
cohort sizes — with **$20M/yr in projected aggregate wage gains** for current
enrollees. Over 80% of the first Arkansas BA cohort stepped immediately into
full-time teaching at **$50,000+, roughly double their prior wages**. In
Jefferson Parish, Louisiana, teacher vacancies were **cut in half**. [^57ce95]
Cohort-wide, aide-to-teacher-of-record wage gains run **65–110%**. [^57ce95]

*(seek chart: single-county story — one district's vacancy line + one cohort's
wage distribution before/after)*

## Beat 5 — One life: Arlie (the story slide)

> **"Arlie spent twenty years as a library aide. Her job became her degree,
> her degree made her a teacher — and her salary doubled."** [^57ce95]

The campaign deck tells her arc in four dates: [^57ce95]

- **2001** — enrolls in college; leaves eighteen months short of her degree
  when family obligations make full-time study impossible.
- **2003–22** — two decades as a classroom aide and a single mom of four:
  in classrooms every day, dreaming of teaching, unable to afford going back.
- **2022** — enrolls at Reach. Keeps her full-time job and benefits; her
  coursework counts her experience and her daily work.
- **2024** — finishes her BA and steps into her own classroom, at roughly
  double her aide salary, **in the district she served for twenty years**.

Read her story the way a rural economist would: for twenty years, the town
had a committed educator it couldn't credential and a wage it couldn't raise.
The fix was never recruiting a teacher from somewhere else — she was already
sitting in the library. Multiply Arlie by the 36% of Reach's candidates who
are rural [^57ce95] and by every education-desert county with a school full
of aides, and Beat 3's mechanism stops being abstract: **the raise is real,
and it never left town.**

*(seek chart: Arlie's timeline 2001 → 2024 with the salary line doubling at
licensure — deliberately the same chart the upward-mobility deck opens with,
so the decks rhyme)*

## Beat 6 — The rural money map (OVERWHELM: the landscape slide)

> **"Rural capital is moving — and it just turned toward education-to-career
> in Reach's exact geography."**

From the 2023 → mid-2026 landscape scan:

| Signal | What happened |
|---|---|
| **Walton Family Foundation** | Home Region Strategy 2030 (May 2026): ~$70M/yr re-centered on education-to-career pathways in NW Arkansas and the **Arkansas–Mississippi Delta** [^5e224c] |
| **MacKenzie Scott's rural wave (2025)** | $50M Native Forward (largest-ever award in Indian Country) [^a14f6b] · $36M to two rural NC community colleges [^496a2b] · $17M Northern Oklahoma College [^333d1c] · $11M Fort Peck tribal college [^b22fe0] · $65M LISC, whose rural program spans 2,000+ counties [^9ec98d] |
| **Ascendium** | $172M awarded in 2025; rural learner success a named strategy — the only durable national rural-postsecondary funder [^63bbab] |
| **Place-based scale** | Dogwood Health Trust: $365M into rural Western NC in 2024–25 [^5126b2] · Lilly GIFT VIII: $312M across all 92 Indiana counties [^e3b418] · Blue Meridian Place Matters: $600M+ [^cca778] |
| **Corporate rural entry** | Goldman Sachs: $100M rural small-business expansion incl. Alabama & Mississippi cohorts [^bfbdb3] · CoBank rural-prosperity grants [^13929c] |

Unrestricted mega-gifts now flow to rural two-year institutions — Reach's
institutional peer set. Place-rooted funders outspend national rural programs
inside their geographies by an order of magnitude. And the freshest signal
points at the Delta.

*(seek chart: funding-flow map of the South — dollars by geography, Delta
highlighted)*

## Beat 7 — Why the Delta, why now (the convergence slide)

> **"Carnegie just funded Reach's Delta expansion. Walton just aimed its
> decade at the Delta. The co-funding table is set."**

Carnegie Corporation of New York committed $2M to expand Reach's teachers
college across the Delta region and launch Mississippi [^0a146a] [^57ce95] — the
first anchor of the $25M growth campaign. Walton's Strategy 2030 names Delta
educator workforce and economic stability as its focus. [^5e224c] A rural
funder joining now co-funds alongside a national anchor, in a geography with a
shovel-ready launch — the highest-leverage seat in rural education philanthropy
this cycle.

## Beat 8 — What your gift catalyzes (the ask)

> **"A place-based income engine that funds itself — your gift buys the
> ignition, not the fuel bill."**

The campaign math: [^57ce95] $25M over 3 years scales 3,400 → 7,500+ enrolled;
recurring district cohorts across LA, AR, AL, TN, CA plus the shovel-ready
Mississippi launch; ~$2,000/candidate/yr of philanthropy against a lean
$8,037/FTE cost structure; **at 7,500 the braided revenue funds the model
permanently.** For a regional funder: a named stake in the Delta launch, next
to Carnegie and Schusterman, with $6M of $25M already committed and the
founding cohort closing August 2026. [^57ce95]

Every dollar's outcome is Arlie's shape (Beat 5): twenty years an aide, two
years a candidate, then teacher-of-record at roughly double the salary — in
the district she never left. [^57ce95]

## Beat 9 — Close

> **"Talent doesn't have to leave to rise. Fund the raise that stays."**
